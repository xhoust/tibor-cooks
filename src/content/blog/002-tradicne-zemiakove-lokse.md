<div class="print-page">
  <h2>Ingredients</h2>
  
  <h3>Potato Dough</h3>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>1000 g</strong> Starchy Russet potatoes, boiled whole in skins until tender, thoroughly cooled and peeled</li>
    <li><strong>250 g–300 g</strong> All-Purpose (AP) flour (unbleached, standard US)</li>
    <li><strong>10 g</strong> Fine sea salt</li>
  </ul>

  <h3>Finishing & Serving</h3>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>80 g–100 g</strong> Rendered duck fat (or goose/pork lard), melted</li>
    <li><strong>To Serve:</strong> These versatile flatbreads can be enjoyed savoury, sweet, or served alongside roast duck and braised cabbage.</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<div class="print-page page-two">
  <div class="print-content-block">
    <h2>Method</h2>
    <ol>
      <li><strong>Prep Potatoes:</strong> Finely grate the cooled, peeled potatoes onto a clean work surface using the fine side of a box grater or pass them through a potato ricer.</li>
      <li><strong>Form Dough:</strong> Scatter the salt and 250 g AP flour over the potatoes. Gently and swiftly knead by hand into a smooth, cohesive dough. Add the remaining flour only if the dough feels overly tacky. Work quickly—resting potato dough causes it to soften and release moisture.</li>
      <li><strong>Portion & Roll:</strong> Divide dough into 10–12 equal portions (approx. 100 g each) and shape into balls. On a well-floured board, roll each ball with a rolling pin into a thin, round disk roughly 2 mm thick (approx. 20–22 cm diameter). Dust excess surface flour off each disk.</li>
      <li><strong>Dry Griddle:</strong> Heat a heavy, dry skillet (cast iron or carbon steel preferred) over medium-high heat on your stovetop without any oil. Place a flatbread in the dry pan and cook for 1–2 minutes until bubbling and dark blister spots appear. Flip and cook 1 minute on the other side.</li>
      <li><strong>Brush & Stack:</strong> Transfer the hot lokša to a warm plate and generously brush the top surface with melted duck fat immediately. Stack subsequent flatbreads directly on top of each other, brushing each layer with fat to keep them soft and pliable.</li>
    </ol>
  </div>

  <div class="tip-at-bottom">
    <blockquote>
      <strong>Chef's Tip:</strong> Always boil potatoes in advance and let them cool completely (preferably chilled overnight); warm potatoes break down quickly into a sticky glue when mixed with flour.
    </blockquote>
  </div>
